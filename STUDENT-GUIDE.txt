CASE RELAY | STUDENT WALKTHROUGH
Security logging, event correlation and investigation
====================================================

Plan for 75–95 minutes, including the written hand-off. You can work at
your own pace, use hints and retry any challenge.

OPEN THE DESK
1. Open index.html in a modern browser or follow your class lab link.
2. Watch the source lanes send log records to the SIEM. Read the four
   short definitions below the diagram.
3. Click “Start the lab.” Use “Resume the lab” when you return later.
4. Read the case brief and the current challenge. Write down the system,
   user and time window when the brief supplies them.
5. Open “Show a hint” for help with the fields to compare.

REPLAY AND EXPLORE
1. Under “SIEM event explorer,” click “Replay arrivals.” When a record
   reaches the SIEM, its source lights up and the replay shows its original
   event time and its arrival time. Gaps are shortened for the replay.
2. Click “Pause replay” to stop automatic advance. “Step arrival” moves
   by one record. “Rewind” resets playback. The full table is available
   at every replay position. Use “Pause motion” to turn off movement.
3. Select a source, search a field value such as a host or account, or enter
   UTC times. Click “Apply filters.” Filters are combined. Time bounds are
   inclusive and apply to the event time for this case day.
4. Use “Sort events” to compare event order with arrival order.
   Use “Clear” to restore the full view.
5. Click an event ID in the table. The inspector shows the source device,
   both UTC times, the original log and a short explanation of the source.
   On smaller screens, the inspector appears below the table.
6. Read the original action, result, user, session and address values.
   Firewall actions describe firewall decisions. Identity and server logs
   report results for the services that generated them.

BUILD YOUR EVIDENCE TIMELINE
1. In Event details, click “Pin event.”
2. Pin each record asked for in the challenge. Check the ID on the card.
3. In an ordered challenge, use “Earlier” and “Later” to arrange cards by
   UTC event time. “Remove” drops one pin. “Clear pins” clears the board.
4. Read “Field matches across your pins.” The board compares common account,
   address, device, session and request fields. Treat matches as leads to
   verify. For example, a public IP may be shared by unrelated devices.
5. Click “Prepare original snapshot” when the challenge asks for it.
   “Download JSON” saves a copy of all originals in the current case with
   their source and timestamps. Your working filters and order do not
   change those originals.

CHECK YOUR FINDING
1. Below the timeline, answer each question under “Check your finding.”
2. Click “Check my work.” Use the feedback to adjust the pins, order or
   responses. If the right records are selected in the wrong order, move
   the cards using their UTC event times.
3. In the AI audit, choose Supported for claims directly backed by the
   source, Contradicted for claims in conflict with it, or Not established
   when the evidence cannot decide. These are supplied training drafts.
4. Click “Next challenge” after a pass. The next challenge starts with a
   clean working board. The top bar and case cards track completions.

WHAT TO PRACTISE
- One red event. Explain what the firewall, identity service and scheduling
  server each know. Plan one follow-up for the real app error.
- A clock out of order. Write down the gateway’s raw time, its UTC time
  and the arrival order. Explain why UTC event time tells a different story.
- The shared address. Compare the users and devices behind one public IP.
  Then identify the stronger relationship in the later identity events.
- The after-hours session. Trace the assigned tunnel address into the
  server and proxy logs. Explain what the byte count and folder operation show.
- Audit the AI draft. Cite the original field for each verdict. Use only
  approved tools and permitted inputs when working with real evidence.
- Final analyst shift. Keep the scheduled backup separate from the other
  transfer. Write a hand-off with confirmed facts, a cautious interpretation,
  unresolved questions, and an owner for follow-up.

HAND-OFF AND CERTIFICATE
1. After completing the last challenge in Final analyst shift, click
   “Go to hand-off.” Write your own brief and download it for discussion.
2. Include the time window, account, session or address and relevant event IDs.
   State what the next analyst should obtain to answer the open questions.
3. Once the header shows “24 / 24 complete,” go to the certificate section,
   enter your name and click “Download PNG.”
4. Use the same browser and lab address when returning. Completed challenges
   are saved in browser storage. Pins and notes are working material. Save
   your downloaded evidence and hand-off before closing the page.
