HARBOUR AFTER DARK
Student walkthrough | Security logging and event correlation

YOUR NIGHT SHIFT
A visitor’s map is late. Later, two desks use the same network and two
deliveries leave for different piers. Piece together what happened and leave
a clear note for the next person on duty. Everything happens in this page.

Start even if you have never used a security tool. A log is a note a device
or service keeps about what it observed. A source is the device or service
that made the note. To “correlate” means to see whether two or more records
fit together. You will practise each idea before combining them.

HOW TO START
1. Open the lab link or index.html. Choose Start my shift. If you have
   already started, choose Resume my shift on the same site and browser.
2. Look at the Harbour projector. Read the sentence beneath the picture.
   The three frames describe one idea. Use the right and left buttons or
   the three dots to move through them at your own speed.
3. Play story runs the three frames. Pause story holds the current frame.
   Replay story starts it again. Pause motion in the top bar switches off
   movement throughout the page. Every frame has a written explanation.
4. Find Evidence slips. Click a numbered slip to open it. Read the ordinary
   sentence first. The smaller original record shows exactly what the source
   wrote. For example, result=printed means the printer finished that job.
5. Read the one decision on your screen. On a phone, scroll down past the
   slips to reach it. Each scene has three decisions.
6. Click Check my answer. Read the explanation. Click Next decision and
   eventually Next scene. There is no timer or penalty for a retry.
7. My logbook shows your written chapter notes. Search the full log desk
   lets you search all 19 case slips. Try a device name, time or slip number.
   Select Save a copy of the sources to download the evidence. Close takes
   you back to the same decision.
8. Shift map shows your progress. Chapters open in order. Come back later
   in the same browser profile at the same address to resume.

WHAT TO CLICK FOR EACH TYPE OF DECISION
Choose one    Click one answer. You can change it before checking.
Pick two      Click two answers. Click a highlighted answer to remove it.
Put in order  Click the earliest event, then the next, then the last.
              Numbers on your selections show your sequence. Remove an
              event by clicking it again. Select it again to put it last.
Read a number Type the number only. The question supplies the unit.
Chapter note  Open the new practice slips. Fill in the three short fields
              using those slips. Next, under “Back to the night watch”,
              return to the original harbour case. Write at least eight
              words answering the question. Click Save my entry.
Give me a hint points you towards a source or a comparison. Each practice
case also has a separate Help with these new slips control.

YOUR ROUTE

CHAPTER 1 | Read a record
Scene 1, The quiet printer. Start with the visitor’s question. Open the print
room slip and look for the reason, waiting time and result. Notice the
difference between reporting a problem and recording how the job ended.
Scene 2, Who saw what? Open the gate reader’s slip. Read each field. Can
it tell you which gate opened? Can it tell you why the person came?
Scene 3, Close the first ticket. Open the ticket and the two printer records.
Order them by time, then pick the records that show completed work.
Practice case, The missing labels. Use the L slips for the three fields.
Return to the P4 printer job for your own note. Name the source and result.

CHAPTER 2 | Fix the clock
Scene 4, Two clock faces. UTC is the common clock used to line up records.
Here the gate is one hour ahead, so a local time of 20:15 would mean 19:15
UTC. Use that operation on the gate time in the case.
Scene 5, The late envelope. Watch which envelope waits. The event time tells
you when the badge worked. The receipt time tells you when the log desk got
the record. Convert to the same clock before subtracting.
Scene 6, Choose the right window. Find the smallest offered period that
includes BOTH target events. Keep the event and receipt times labelled.
Practice case, The footbridge clock. Use its own two-hour offset. Enter a
time as HH:MM and minutes as a number. For the chapter note, switch back
to the original gate and explain the late arrival.

CHAPTER 3 | Follow the right desk
Scene 7, One Wi-Fi, two people. A sign-in summary can count several failures
in one record. Look for count. Compare the account on each successful login.
Scene 8, Follow the address. Remote access lends a connected desk an inside
address. Compare assigned on the session slip with client on the file slip.
Then check the account too.
Scene 9, Test a second explanation. Order the failures and successes. Look
for what differs between the two successful sessions even on the same Wi-Fi.
Practice case, Who held the address? An address can be given to a different
desk later. Check who held it at the time of the file read. In your note,
go back to Nia’s session and give two matching details.

CHAPTER 4 | Check the delivery
Scene 10, Read the delivery permit. Open the change book. A permit says who
may send, where the delivery may go, and when. Compare all three conditions.
MB (megabyte) measures how much data travelled.
Scene 11, Put the rule to work. Answer the first decision. On the second
decision, find Try the permit checker. Select Time alone and click Test
these checks. Read the three results. Turn on Sender and Recipient as well
and click Test these checks again. Compare what changed. Then pick the two
conditions the North Pier delivery missed. Finish the final time example.
Scene 12, Which way did it go? Watch the outward and return journeys. Read
sent_MB for data leaving the desk and received_MB for data coming back.
The traffic desk counted data. Check whether it recorded the item name.
Practice case, A second permit. Use the P slips to find a wrong destination
and a late delivery. In your note, go back to ticket H4 and North Pier.

CHAPTER 5 | Write the handover
Scene 13, Check the AI’s story. Read the draft, then check each claim against
the systems that saw the activity. One source names a file. Another source
counts data. Think about what it would take to prove the file was sent.
Scene 14, Ask the right person. A ticket owner can confirm the scope of a
permit. A receiving service might have a record of the object it received.
Choose a focused enquiry for the right owner and time.
Scene 15, Sign the morning report. Trace the same desk through connection,
file read and transfer. Separate the confirmed details from open questions.
Practice case, The draft that guessed. Use the R slips for the three checks.
For your final harbour note, use this optional sentence starter:
“The records show ____. We still need to learn ____. Ask ____ to check ____.”

FINISH
After saving the last chapter entry, choose Finish my shift. Enter the name
you want on the certificate. Download certificate (PNG) saves an image.
Download shift report (TXT) saves your five notes, practice answers and
decision explanations for your instructor. Keep those downloads for class.
If you return later, View my completed shift opens these downloads again.

IF YOU ARE STUCK
Check the unit, the time label and the slip number. In a chapter practice
case, use its lettered slips for the short fields and the numbered night
watch slips for your paragraph. If the permit checker asks for another
trial, look at its two “Trials recorded” marks. Previous decision lets you
reread a signed answer. Shift map and Resume my shift return you to the
first unfinished step.
